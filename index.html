Letterpress math

The compositor's trade ran on exact arithmetic - points, picas, sheet fractions - and the modern hobby press runs on guessing. A page is a fixed depth of metal, a sheet is a fixed field of paper, and ink is a film measured in milligrams.

Set the stick

The problem it solves

Every letterpress job meets the same three walls: the text that ends one line into the next page, the parent sheet that yields nine cards when sixteen were planned, and the ink that runs out two hundred impressions early. Letterpress math answers all three from the numbers you actually have: page depth, type size and leading; sheet and card dimensions; impressions, area and coverage.

What it does not do

The arithmetic is exact; the norms are not. The leading bands, waste verdicts and the ink film figure are flagged craft conventions, and the ink card is a labeled estimate, not a measurement of your ink, paper or press. It plans the run - the proof still comes off the press.
